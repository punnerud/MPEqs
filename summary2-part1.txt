  n = 3 893   peak scratch  1.5 MiB   against    57.8 MiB dense

(`matstruct knn --verify` cross-checks against geodesic.rs::build_knn).
This unlocks the scale every graph result so far was blocked on — the 39.08 %
exact-cell finding had only ever been measured on a 4 000-point subsample.

Two bugs found and fixed while building it, both of the same kind:

1. ANGLE FROM DOT PRODUCT IS CATASTROPHIC CANCELLATION.
   The first implementation valued distances as 2*atan2(sqrt(2-2*dot), ...).
   That is acos's conditioning problem relocated, not solved: measured slack at
   the self-distance was 0.69 quantisation units, and anything above 0.5 rounds
   to 1. Since every criterion downstream is integer equality of a residual,
   this would have corrupted exactly the quantity being measured.
   FIX: rank by the dot product (monotone, blocked, cheap), then value the k
   survivors per row with componentwise differences. n*k accurate angles instead
   of n^2. This is what the module comment claimed and the code did not do.

2. pick_landmarks ALLOCATED NEARLY n^2.
   cand_cap = 0 meant "every point", making the endpoint x candidate table
   min(n, 8192) x n = 1.6 GB at n = 50 000 — in a function whose doc comment
   said it never touches n^2.
   FIX: default to MTZU's own rule, (n/4).clamp(256, 1024), bounding it at 33 MB.

   The cap COSTS QUALITY and that is now a test, not a footnote:
   `capping_candidates_costs_quality` asserts uncapped > capped on the gateway
   world. MPEE records the same direction (London tol-5s block share 6 % at 192
   candidates vs 32 % at 1024). The gateway validation now passes an explicit
   uncapped cap so it still validates against MPEE's measured behaviour.

Tests: 23 pass, clippy -D warnings clean.

PHASE 3 — MORE HOPS: PREDICTION FALSIFIED                            [COMPLETE]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The plan predicted, in writing: "3-hop helps on the angular metric and not on
the geodesic." Min-plus squarings of the hub table:

  configuration          cells%  recall@10  resid
  angular,   sq=0/1/2      0.89      0.097  0.714   (identical at every squaring)
  geodesic,  sq=0          27.92      0.283  0.078
  geodesic,  sq=1/2        27.90      0.283  0.078

WRONG, and the reasoning was wrong in an instructive way. I argued that because
the chord "does not route through anything", a chain of hubs might beat the
direct hub-to-hub entry. But the triangle inequality guarantees the direct chord
is already <= any detour, and the angular metric IS a metric. Min-plus closure is
idempotent on ANY metric, not merely on shortest-path metrics.

So MPEE's argument for stopping at two hubs is more general than they state it.
It is not "dhh is already a shortest path"; it is "dhh is already a metric
distance". Multi-hop can only pay when the hub table is an APPROXIMATION of the
true distance, and here it is exact by construction.

The junctions describe the metric well and search it badly. Those are different
jobs and this thread has been conflating them.

A STRUCTURAL FINDING CLIPPY FOUND, NOT ME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"this `if` has identical blocks" in the path miner. The out- and in-direction
scored `d(i,a) + d(p,a)` and `d(p,a) + d(i,a)` — the same expression.

MPEE keeps the two sides separate because road networks are DIRECTED: with
one-way streets a point's best on-ramp is not its best off-ramp. Every metric
here is symmetric, so the two credit histograms are identical and mining twice
produces two copies of one answer.

So on a symmetric metric the two-sided model DEGENERATES TO ONE-SIDED while
still costing twice the resident memory. That is a large part of why it loses to
landmark.rs here and wins on MPEE's directed matrices, and it was sitting in a
lint I had been treating as a style note.

PHASE 6 — THE ALGEBRA WAS WRONG: CHAINS OF STRONG LINKS
================================================================================
Objection raised, and it is correct: everything above minimised a SUM of
distances. Under (min, +) the triangle inequality guarantees the direct edge
beats any detour, so of course extra hops did nothing — that result answers the
wrong question. What matters is a chain where EVERY STEP is strong, which is not
capped by any triangle inequality because similarities multiply.

New: experiments/hops/chains.py. Algebra (max, x) over similarity in [0,1]:

    strength(chain) = product of link similarities  x  damping^(hops-1)
    S_k(i,j)        = max over chains of at most k hops

Two design points came from the objection and both changed the result:
  - RELATIVE, not absolute. Each node's links are divided by its own strongest,
    and only links within 10 % of it are kept. Absolute cutoffs keep everything
    in dense regions and nothing in sparse ones, so every chain found would be a
    chain through the dense part.
  - DON'T SEARCH AMONG WEAK LINKS. That floor is what stops kNN from admitting
    neighbours that are only nearest by default.

THE GROUND TRUTH I CHOSE FIRST WAS THE WRONG ONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I scored chains on whether they link SAME-CLASS pairs, and reported 0.86x falling
to 0.11x as failure. That is backwards. A link between two items already known to
be alike carries no information — it is redundant by construction, and high
same-class purity is what a USELESS discovery looks like. Fetching knowledge
across domains looks like an error to that metric and is the whole point.

Rescored as: does the link help SOLVE something. One-step label propagation,
direct links against direct + chain:

  hops  accuracy  rescued  broken    net   cross%  cross-only acc
     1     0.947        -       -      -        -               -
     2     0.908       25     142   -117    24.1%           0.718
     3     0.835       39     376   -337    58.2%           0.413
     6     0.458       33    1500  -1467    90.6%           0.013

The chains DO rescue items the direct graph gets wrong, and the count is stable
at 33-39 all the way out to six hops. That is real transfer, not zero. It is also
swamped: 4x to 45x more items are broken than rescued.

ADMITTING ONLY THE STRONGEST CHAINS — AND IT STILL DOES NOT PAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  hops  keep top   links  cross%   rescued  broken   net
     2     50.0%   65454   17.9%        34      78   -44
     2     10.0%   13092   12.6%        21      27    -6
     2      1.0%    2428   11.0%         5       5    +0
     3     10.0%   24010   34.1%        28      87   -59
     3      1.0%    2402   27.0%        10      15    -5

Net converges to zero FROM BELOW and never turns positive. At the tightest
threshold rescued equals broken exactly. So the negative is not an artefact of
searching among weak links — the sweep covers 50 % down to 0.1 % — and not an
artefact of demanding same-class, since the score rewards cross-domain rescue.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Settled: on these embeddings, chains of strong links carry no NET transferable
information. Every strength threshold trades one rescue for at least one break.

NOT settled, and the limitation is real: direct label propagation already scores
0.947, so only 5.3 % of items are available to rescue while 94.7 % are available
to break. The measurement is dominated by breakage of what already works. A task
with genuine headroom — where the direct graph is wrong often — would be a fairer
test of transfer, and this one cannot distinguish "chains carry nothing" from
"chains carry something that this task has no room to show".

PHASE 7 — THE CHAINS THEMSELVES, AND WHAT QWEN SAYS ABOUT THEM
================================================================================
The aggregate could count that chains break more than they rescue. It could not
say why. Printing the paths does, and the two examples invert the expected order.

MATCHING (arithmetic -> physics), chain strength 0.4910
  721 + 935  ->  945 + 690  ->  910 + 351  ->  277 + 351
             ->  P = 277 J / 6 s  ->  P = 292 J / 24 s  ->  P = 293 J / 15 s
  link strengths: 1.000, 0.982, 1.000, 0.941, 1.000, 0.999

NOT MATCHING (arithmetic -> prose), chain strength 0.5256
  -694 - 435  ->  -694 - 262  ->  213 - 262
              ->  "number 22 is to honor Clark"  ->  cemeteries at Le Treport
              ->  the depiction of Pentecost  ->  secret observation posts
  link strengths: 1.000, 0.996, 0.994, 0.999, 1.000, 1.000

THE UNRELATED CHAIN IS THE STRONGER OF THE TWO — 0.5256 against 0.4910. Chain
strength is anti-correlated with meaning here, which is the aggregate's negative
result made visible in one pair.

The mechanism is legible in the path. Both chains cross domains on a LEXICAL
coincidence: `277 + 351` reaches `P = 277 J / 6 s` because the numeral 277 is in
both, and `213 - 262` reaches "number 22 is to honor Clark" through digits and
the word "number". And once a chain enters prose every onward link is ~1.000,
because prose chunks all resemble each other relative to their own best — the
relative floor cannot discriminate inside a homogeneous domain, so the chain
wanders freely at no cost. That is the mechanism behind "breaks more than it
rescues", and it took the paths to see it.

QWEN'S VERDICT (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, IQ1_M)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  matching chain     "NO. The first item is simple arithmetic addition, while the
                      last involves physics power calculations; they share no
                      transferable knowledge."
  not-matching chain "NO. The last item concerns secret observation posts, while
                      the first is an arithmetic calculation."
  the pivot itself   "It is a surface coincidence. The numbers match, but the
   277+351 <->        mathematical operations and physical meanings differ
   P = 277 J / 6 s    fundamentally."

Asked only whether the pair was meaningfully similar, Qwen named the exact
mechanism unprompted: the bridge is the shared numeral.

CAVEAT ON THIS EVIDENCE. IQ1_M is aggressive 1-bit quantisation and an LLM
judgement is not a measurement. It is reported because it agrees independently
with two things established without it — the net transfer of 0 at every strength
threshold, and the shared-token bridge visible in the printed path. Three signals
of different kinds pointing the same way is worth more than any one of them.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The failure is not that chains are a bad idea; it is that these embeddings put
`277 + 351` next to `P = 277 J / 6 s`. A representation that encoded what a
problem IS rather than which tokens it contains would give chains something real
to walk along. That is the same conclusion the domain measurements reached from
the other side — embeddings resolve category at 0.974 purity and within-category
structure at 1.15x — and it is why the chain crosses domains only where the
surface accidentally agrees.

PHASE 8 — TWO OBJECTIONS THAT BOTH LANDED
================================================================================

OBJECTION 1: "links of 1.000 and 0.982 — isn't that a very strong connection?"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No, and the question exposed a design fault. Those were RELATIVE values — each
link divided by its own node's strongest — so 1.000 means "my best neighbour"
whatever the absolute distance. Checked against the absolute similarity:

  chain          hop  relative  ABSOLUTE  angle
  matching       1-6   0.94-1.00  0.725-0.837  0.51-0.86 rad
  not matching   3     0.994      0.603        1.248 rad   <- crossing into prose
  not matching   4-6   ~1.000     0.606-0.643  ~1.2 rad

The chain crossed into prose on a link 1.248 radians wide — near-orthogonal, out
of a maximum of pi/2 = 1.571 — and scored it 0.994 because a prose chunk's best
neighbour is barely closer than random. Relative normalisation made regions
comparable, as intended, and destroyed the absolute information along with it.

And the fix reverses the earlier result: on ABSOLUTE strength the matching chain's
weakest link is 0.725 and the unrelated one's is 0.603, so the ordering comes out
RIGHT. The inversion reported in phase 7 was an artefact of my own normalisation.

OBJECTION 2: "you cannot multiply them, cf. exploding gradients"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Correct. A product of similarities compounds — it decays exponentially with real
values, and normalising them towards 1 removes the decay along with the signal.
Replaced with (max, min): a chain is exactly as strong as its weakest link, with
a per-hop cost SUBTRACTED and clipped at zero, so nothing compounds and nothing
exceeds 1.

Net transfer at the tighter thresholds went from negative to zero:
  2 hops, keep top 10 %:  -6  ->  +0
  2 hops, keep top  1 %:  -6  ->  +0
  3 hops, keep top 10 %: -59  -> -22
Still never positive, but no longer harmful, and correlation to the first hop
degrades gracefully (0.898, 0.765, 0.576, 0.325) instead of collapsing to -0.348.

WEIGHTING BY HOW OFTEN A DIMENSION FIRES — the proposal, and the trade it hits
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"One that always fires gets damped, few times gets stronger" is inverse document
frequency on dimensions. It does exactly what was asked of it and costs more than
it saves:

  weighting                separation   vs cosine   numeral bridge
  none (raw extreme)           +0.311        2.6x            2.221
  rarity (idf)                 +0.087        0.7x            0.734  <- damped 67 %
  peakedness (kurtosis)        +0.000        0.0x            0.040
  concentration (gini)         +0.309        2.6x            2.050

IDF damps the spurious bridge by 67 %, exactly as intended — dim 218 fires on 221
of 3200 items, so it is discounted. But domain separation collapses to below
cosine, because a dimension that MARKS a domain fires on that whole domain, and
frequency cannot tell "fires on everything" from "fires on one whole domain".
Concentration measures (gini, kurtosis) do not rescue it: gini keeps separation
but does not damp the bridge, kurtosis destroys both.

THE TRADE IS REAL AND IT IS NOT A TUNING PROBLEM. What makes a dimension useful
for separating domains is the same property that makes it available for a
spurious bridge: it fires strongly across a broad class. The numeral dimension is
not a bug to filter out — it is a domain marker being used correctly, on two items
that genuinely share the property it marks. The problem is that "contains 277" is
a real shared property and a useless one, and no reweighting of the embedding can
know the difference, because the embedding never encoded it.

================================================================================
PHASE 10 — "RELATIVE TO EVERYTHING, BECAUSE IT FIRES ON EVERYTHING WHEN CORRECT"
================================================================================
That is the resolution IDF could not reach. A dimension marking one domain fires
on that whole domain, and firing on all of it is CORRECT behaviour, so frequency
must not be the penalty. What distinguishes a marker from a stopword is whether
the set it fires on is itself coherent.

COHERENCE, label-free: of the items where a dimension fires, how similar are they
to each other, above the population mean?

  weighting                separation   vs cosine   numeral bridge
  cosine                       +0.118        1.0x                -
  none (raw extreme)           +0.324        2.7x            2.221
  rarity (idf)                 +0.090        0.8x            0.734
  COHERENCE                    +0.275        2.3x            1.022

Coherence keeps 2.3x of cosine's separation AND damps the spurious bridge by
54 %. IDF could only do the second, and paid by falling below cosine.

The numbers show exactly why frequency cannot do this job:

  dim 218 (the numeral bridge)  fires on 221   coherence 0.16
  dim 130 (a physics marker)    fires on 332   coherence 1.00
  dim 239                       fires on 207   coherence 0.86
  dim 105 (a physics marker)    fires on 201   coherence 0.82

Same frequency band, opposite usefulness. IDF sees 221 against 201 and treats
them alike; coherence sees 0.16 against 0.82 and does not.

CLOSING: WHAT THE OBJECTIONS CHANGED
================================================================================
Five objections were raised during this work and four of them overturned a result
I had already written down:

1. "why are we talking about SHORTER?"        -> min-plus was the wrong algebra;
                                                 the whole "more hops does nothing"
                                                 result answered the wrong question
2. "don't search among weak links, adjust
    relatively"                                -> gave the relative floor, which then
                                                 caused failure 3
3. "links of 1.000 — isn't that very strong?" -> no: relative scoring rated a
                                                 1.248-radian link at 0.994, and
                                                 fixing it REVERSED phase 7's finding
4. "you cannot multiply them"                 -> product replaced by bottleneck;
                                                 net transfer went from negative to 0
5. "are some of the numbers strong?"          -> shared extreme dimensions separate
                                                 domains 2.7x better than cosine, the
                                                 best similarity found in this work
6. "relative to everything, because it fires
    on everything when correct"                -> coherence weighting, which is the
                                                 only one that keeps separation and
                                                 damps the spurious bridge together

The pattern is worth naming. Every one of my errors was the same kind: I picked a
normalisation or an aggregator for a good local reason and did not check what it
destroyed. Relative scoring made regions comparable and erased absolute strength.
Products expressed "every step matters" and compounded. IDF damped stopwords and
silenced domain markers. In each case the fix was not a better parameter but a
different quantity, and in each case the measurement that would have caught it
early was available and not taken.

================================================================================
PHASE 11 — "STILL USEFUL FOR FINDING OTHER RELATED THINGS, AS HOPS ONWARD"
================================================================================
Fair, and I had been measuring the wrong use. Every transfer number above came
from ADDING all chain-found links to the graph and checking whether prediction
improved — automatic substitution. Nobody proposed that. The proposal was to use
chains to FIND things worth looking at, which is a candidate generator, and a
candidate generator is scored on coverage and ranking, not on blind inclusion.

Rescored that way, on 3 000 points where direct label propagation is wrong on 156:

              2 hops   3 hops
  wrong items reached by chains      153/156    155/156
  precision over all candidates        0.015      0.017
  precision in the top 50 by strength  0.000      0.000
  total candidates                   130 906    240 052

COVERAGE IS ESSENTIALLY COMPLETE. Chains reach 153 of the 156 items the direct
graph gets wrong, and 155 of 156 at three hops. Whatever is wrong with the
mechanism, failing to find things is not it.

RANKING IS WHAT IS MISSING. Precision is 1.5 %, and sorting by bottleneck strength
does not concentrate the useful ones — the top 50 contains none. That is precisely
why blind inclusion hurt: 130 906 links admitted to obtain 1 942 useful ones.

So the honest statement is not "chains do not work". It is:

    The mechanism has the COVERAGE a discovery tool needs and lacks the RANKING.

Those are different failures with different fixes. Missing coverage would mean the
graph cannot reach the answer and the approach is dead. Missing ranking means the
answer is in the list and the list is too long, which is a scoring problem — and
phase 10 produced a scoring signal that chain strength is not: coherence-weighted
shared-extreme dimensions, which separate domains 2.3x better than cosine and halve
the spurious bridges. Whether that ranks these candidates is the obvious next
measurement and has not been made.

WHAT THIS THREAD ACTUALLY DELIVERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Positive, measured, and reproducible:
  - streaming kNN: 50 000 points at 308 MB and 49 s, verified identical to dense
  - betweenness hubs beat facility location 1.45x-4.63x, growing with n
  - shared-extreme dimensions beat cosine 2.7x at domain separation, and say WHICH
    dimension made the link — cosine cannot
  - coherence weighting keeps 2.3x of that while halving spurious bridges
  - chains reach 155 of 156 items a direct graph gets wrong

Negative, and equally measured:
  - two-sided labels tie or lose to one relay on symmetric metrics, at 2x memory
  - more hops does nothing under min-plus, and the reason is general
  - chain strength does not rank candidates: top-50 precision is zero
  - net transfer from blind inclusion is 0 at best, at every threshold tried

================================================================================
PHASE 12 — THE APPLICATION: A SMALL MODEL WORKING WITHOUT LOOPING
================================================================================
The proposal: keep the strongest links as a graph, give a small distilled model
external working memory, and let it grind through many steps — physics, maths,
parsing a document — without looping and without destroying what it has done.

Measured the premise before building it. OLMoE 1B-7B, 8 multi-step arithmetic
problems with checkable answers, its own output fed back, up to 6 steps.

THREE HARNESS BUGS ON THE WAY, EACH ONE NEARLY A FINDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The first run reported 28 repeats across 7 of 8 tasks — a 58 % loop rate, which
   would have justified the whole design. Every one was my line extractor taking
   ```python as the "step" and then seeing it again. With fences stripped: 2
   repeats across 2 of 8.
2. The enforced `name = expression = number` store rejected 48 of 48 lines. Not
   the model refusing the format — my extractor returning the stray token "1".
3. Only after printing completely unprocessed output did the real behaviour show.

WHAT THE MODEL ACTUALLY DOES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Asked for one step at a time, it does not decompose at all. It writes a Python
block for the WHOLE expression, then fabricates an `output` block, then states
DONE with the fabricated number:

    result = (120 / 4 + 15) * 2 - 30      <- correct
    ```output
    90.0                                   <- invented; true value 60
    DONE: 90

So the failure is neither of the two the memory was aimed at:

  loops                      2 of 41 steps
  operations with no result  20 of 41 steps  (real, but downstream of the above)
  refuses to decompose       8 of 8 tasks
  fabricates evaluation      every task that reached an answer

THE MISSING COMPONENT IS AN EVALUATOR, NOT A MEMORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The model CAN express the computation and CANNOT evaluate it. Executing the
expression it writes, instead of trusting the output it claims:

  stepwise, free-form history          0/8
  stepwise, enforced value store       0/8
  expression written and EVALUATED     6/8      <- expression written for 7/8

Zero to six, from running the arithmetic rather than believing it. The two
failures are both transcription, not arithmetic: on `(120/4 + 15) * 2 - 30` it
wrote `120 / 4 + 15 * 2 - 30`, losing the parentheses, and one problem produced no
parseable expression at all.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE GRAPH MEMORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It is aimed at a failure this model does not have, on this class of task. A loop
detector would have caught 2 steps in 41. The state store had nothing to store
because the model never emits intermediate results. What it is short of is a way
to check its own arithmetic, and that is a tool call, not a memory.

That is a statement about THIS task, and the honest limit of it is that
single-expression arithmetic can be one-shotted, so it never needs memory. Parsing
a long document cannot: the state does not fit in context, revisiting is real, and
"what have I already extracted" is a genuine question. The graph memory should be
tested there, where the premise is not satisfiable by a one-line expression — and
the premise itself should be measured first, as it was here, because on this task
the answer was that the premise did not hold.

================================================================================
PHASE 13 — PEN AND PAPER: ONE TINY OPERATION, WITH THE GRAPH CHECKING
================================================================================
Corrected design: the evaluator runs SMALL PIECES, not generated Python. The model
proposes one binary operation on two values already written down; we evaluate that
one operation; the result AND the transition are kept; the graph checks that every
step derives from the pad and that every number in the problem gets consumed.

Built as specified: append-only pad, derivation check, coverage check, refusals
fed back so the model can rationalise rather than repeat.

A FIFTH HARNESS BUG, AND IT EXPLAINED ALL FOUR EARLIER ONES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every strange output in this experiment — markdown fences, a stray "1", a bare
operator, empty completions — had one cause: OLMoE is an INSTRUCT model and I was
running it as raw completion with `-no-cnv`, outside the chat template it was
trained in. With `<|user|>` / `<|assistant|>` applied it immediately produced
structured steps.

A model asked outside its format does not fail gracefully. It fails
unrecognisably, and each failure looks like a different finding about the model.
Four separate "results" in this experiment were that one mistake wearing costumes.

WHAT THE GRAPH CATCHES — AND IT IS EXACTLY THE NAMED FAILURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Watched proposal by proposal on `((15 + 25) / 8) * 14 - 20`:

    available: 15, 25, 8, 14, 20
    proposal 1: 15 + 25   -> accepted, v1 = 40
    proposal 2: 15 + 25   -> REFUSED, 15 is no longer available
    proposal 3: 15 + 25   -> REFUSED
    proposal 4: 15 + 25   -> REFUSED

The model takes the first step correctly and then proposes it forever, while the
pad plainly shows `v1 = 15 + 25 = 40` and the available values are `40, 8, 14, 20`.
This is "suddenly thinking anew without rationalising it" and its opposite at once
— it is not thinking anew at all, it is stuck — and THE GRAPH DETECTS IT EVERY
TIME. 58 underivable steps refused across 8 problems.

RESULTS, AGAINST THE SAME MODEL AND PROBLEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  free-form stepwise history                    0/8
  enforced named value store                    0/8
  scratchpad + derivation graph                 0/8   (58 refusals, 1/8 covered)
  scratchpad + graph + refusals fed back        0/8   (no change)
  ONE whole expression, externally evaluated    6/8

The architecture works and the model cannot drive it. Detection is not the
bottleneck: the graph caught every repeat and every underivable step. Choosing the
NEXT step is the bottleneck, and feeding the refusals back did not move it — at
1B active parameters the model has one good move in it and no second.

The sharpest form of the result: this model can plan the whole computation in one
shot (7/8 expressions written correctly, 6/8 evaluating correctly) and cannot
perform the same computation incrementally (0/8). Incremental is precisely the mode
external memory exists to support, and it is the mode this model cannot enter.

WHAT THAT DOES AND DOES NOT SAY ABOUT THE DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It does not say the design is wrong. The pad, the derivation check and the
coverage check all did their jobs, and they are the parts that would matter on a
task too large to one-shot — parsing a long document, where the state does not fit
in context and "what have I already extracted" is a real question.

It says the design needs a model that can take a second step. Every failure here
was upstream of the memory: the model could not choose, so the memory had nothing
to be useful about. Testing the same harness with a larger model is the measurement
that separates "the architecture does not help" from "this model cannot use it",
and those are very different conclusions.

================================================================================
PHASE 14 — THE WORK RECORD: READ A ROW, PATCH WITHOUT REWRITING, NEVER RETRY BLIND
================================================================================
Built to the spec: read row by row so only the current step and the next need holding,
append derived rows, patch a row in place without touching anything before it, and
keep a memory of what has been explored OUTSIDE the model.

`experiments/loopmem/workpad.py`, 25 tests, all passing. Verified against a SCRIPTED
agent that makes deliberate mistakes, not against a model — phase 13 showed store
bugs and model limits are different failures, and mixing them makes each look like
the other.

What it enforces:
  DERIVATION    an operand must exist, be live, and not already be consumed
  PROPAGATION   patching row 5 marks rows 6, 9, 14 stale transitively; nothing
                derived from a corrected value is left quietly standing
  APPEND-ONLY   nothing before the patched row is touched, and a superseded row
                stays readable with its transition intact
  MEMORY        every attempt and its outcome is recorded and never deleted

TWO DESIGN ERRORS, THE SECOND ONE CAUGHT BY THE OBJECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
First: I keyed the explored set on row ids alone. The end-to-end demo then did this:

    step 1 (wrong): row 5 = 120 - 4 = 116
    step 2:         row 6 = [5] + [2] = 131        built on the error
    patch row 5 to '/'  ->  30, row 6 marked STALE
    retry [5] + [2]     ->  REFUSED, "already tried"

That refusal is wrong. `[5] + [2]` after the patch computes 30 + 15, not 116 + 15 —
a different computation on a corrected premise. Blocking it makes the fix useless,
which is exactly "new knowledge can open up trying what was wrong with a different
approach".

Fixed by versioning: every row carries a version, patching bumps it and the versions
of all its descendants, and the explored key includes operand versions. So
  - an exact repeat on the same premise is still refused
  - the same step on a CORRECTED premise is allowed
  - the old attempt is not deleted; `history()` still answers "this was tried before,
    on a different premise"

That last part is the difference between a memory and a blocklist.

Second: two tests then failed, and they were asserting the OLD behaviour — that a
patched step could never be retried. They were rewritten to assert the corrected
behaviour, with a note in the file saying the reversal was deliberate. A test that
encodes a bug will defend the bug.

WHERE THIS LEAVES THE WHOLE THREAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The store is built and verified. It does everything the design asks: sequential
reading, bounded working set, non-destructive patching, transitive invalidation,
and a persistent record of what has been explored that a fresh context cannot lose.

It has not been shown to help a model, and on the evidence from phase 13 the 1B
model cannot drive it — it solved 0/8 with the scratchpad and 6/8 by writing one
whole expression we executed. The store's value has to be demonstrated on a task
that cannot be one-shotted, with a model that can take a second step, and both of
those are measurements not yet made.

What can be said now: the mechanism is correct, its failure modes are the ones it
was designed to catch, and the two bugs found in building it were both cases of a
rule that was right in general being wrong after a correction.

================================================================================
PHASE 15 — "A MODEL SHOULD BE ABLE TO ITERATE LONG WITHOUT DESTROYING THE ANSWER"
================================================================================
The central claim of the design, and it is a claim about the RECORD rather than the
driver — so it can be tested with a scripted proposer instead of waiting for a
better model. That was the right instinct and the experiment still failed to test
it, for a reason worth writing down.

WHAT THE ATTEMPT FOUND, INCLUDING ABOUT ITSELF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two real bugs in the driver, both instances of the same design point:

1. A record arm scored exactly 0.000 at EVERY budget. Cause: the driver could only
   append. Four appends consume all five givens, the answer is wrong, and the loop
   stops — so the budget was never spent and "iterating longer" never happened.
   Protection without a way back is not protection, it is paralysis. `undo()` was
   added to the record for this, and it is now part of the tested interface.

2. Reaching a dead end still ended the run rather than triggering a step back. A
   dead end is not an ending; it is the one moment backtracking exists for.

And one flaw in the comparison itself. The naive arm ends by naming its most recent
value, so it always asserts an answer and is right by coincidence about a fifth of
the time. The record asserts only when a complete derivation exists. Reporting
0.219 against 0.019 would have compared "did the target number appear anywhere" to
"was a valid derivation built", which are not the same question.

WHY THE EXPERIMENT HAS NO POWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rebuilt to compare claim rate against precision, all three arms sit at ~0.00
precision at every budget from 10 to 400. A random proposer essentially never
solves a five-given problem: order matters, four operators, and only a handful of
the orderings are right. With a base rate of zero there is no difference to detect,
and monotonicity over a flat line of zeros means nothing.

So the claim is NOT tested. A random driver is too weak a stand-in for "thinks a
lot", and the honest statement is that this experiment lacked the power to measure
what it was built to measure.

WHAT IS ESTABLISHED, AND WHAT WOULD ESTABLISH THE REST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Verified, by 25 tests against a scripted agent making deliberate mistakes:

  - a correction never touches anything before it
  - everything derived from a corrected value is marked stale, transitively
  - a repeat on the same premise is refused and remembered
  - a correction REOPENS what it invalidated, while the old attempt stays on record
  - a dead end can be stepped back from without losing the memory of what failed

Those are structural guarantees: the record cannot silently destroy an established
result, because there is no operation that overwrites one. That is the part of the
claim that can be settled by construction, and it is settled.

The part that cannot: whether a driver iterating longer actually gets better. That
needs a driver with a success rate above zero, which means a model that can choose
a second step — and phase 13 measured that this 1B model cannot, scoring 0/8
stepwise against 6/8 writing one whole expression. Two measurements would settle
it, neither yet made: the same harness with a larger model, and a task that cannot
be one-shotted so that iteration is forced rather than optional.

================================================================================
PHASE 16 — CONFIDENCE FROM THE GRAPH: DOES MULTI-PATH SUPPORT MEAN ANYTHING?
================================================================================
The proposal that closes the loop between the two threads: use the N x N structure
as a SAFETY measure rather than as a retrieval index — score each step and check
whether its values are actually strongly supported, instead of accepting whatever
the driver proposes. If support ranks good steps first, a weak driver is protected
by the record and phase 15's missing base rate appears.

The signal available without a model is multi-path support: enumerate the
derivations a set of givens admits and count how many independent routes reach each
value. Exhaustive, 200 000 routes per problem.

  problem                     distinct values   answer support   top support   rank
  (120 / 4 + 15) * 2 - 30              13 792              335         1 897     51
  ((9 * 7) + (8 * 6)) - 51             18 032               25         1 638  1 747
  (200 - 45 * 3) / 5 + 12              21 783               74           618    183
  ((15 + 25) / 8) * 14 - 20            20 060               37           868    994
  (6 * 6 + 4 * 4) / 13 + 7              7 824              136         2 508    266
  ((100 - 28) / 6) * 5 - 30            20 099               17           960  2 348
  (81 / 9 + 11) * 3 - 40               22 111                8           619  5 313

  best-supported value IS the answer:  0/8
  answer among the five best supported: 0/8

FLATLY NEGATIVE, and the reason generalises. In free arithmetic over a set of
numbers every value is reachable many ways, and the best-supported ones are those
easiest to hit by accident — small integers, round numbers — not the correct ones.
SUPPORT MEASURES REACHABILITY, NOT TRUTH.

Multi-path agreement is a confidence signal only when the competing paths are
constrained to be derivations of the SAME claim. Here they are derivations of
different claims, and counting them measures how easy a number is to make. The
answer to `(81/9 + 11) * 3 - 40` has 8 routes; the most-reachable value has 619.

================================================================================
CLOSING: WHAT THE GRAPH GIVES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
================================================================================
Across sixteen phases the same shape recurred. The graph machinery reliably gives
COVERAGE and DETECTION and never once gave RANKING or TRUTH:

  coverage    chains reach 155 of 156 items a direct graph gets wrong
  detection   58 underivable steps refused; every repeat caught; transitive
              invalidation verified by 25 tests
  structure   shared-extreme dimensions separate domains 2.7x better than cosine,
              and say WHICH dimension did it
  ranking     chain strength: top-50 precision 0.000
  truth       multi-path support: answer ranks 51st to 5313th, 0/8 first

Every attempt to turn the structure into a judgement failed, and each failed for a
different and specific reason: chain strength ranks by reachability, IDF cannot
tell a domain marker from a stopword, support counts how easy a value is to reach.
The structure is real — 31x gateway concentration, 39 % of distances exact from
0.8 % of points, 155/156 coverage — and it says where things are, never whether
they are right.

That is a coherent place to stop. A discovery tool that finds nearly everything and
ranks nothing is useful with a judge attached and misleading without one. What this
work has built is the finding half, verified; what it has repeatedly failed to
build, by six different routes, is the judging half.

================================================================================
PHASE 17 — even 13 + 8 is too big a step
================================================================================

The scratchpad used one binary operation on two multi-digit numbers as its atom and a
1B model solved 0 of 8. The suggestion was that the atom was still too coarse: 13 + 8
is not one operation, it is 3 + 8 = 11, write 1, carry 1, then 1 + 1 = 2. The carry is
the join, and the carry is exactly where this project had already measured arithmetic
to stop being decomposable — held-out accuracy fell 0.647 to 0.437 as carries went 0
to 3, and grouping training data by carry count was worth +0.157 to +0.226, the
largest verified effect in the whole project.

Twelve carry-heavy three-digit additions, OLMoE 1B-7B, three arms:

    whole number in one go   6/12
    one column at a time    11/12
    columns done correctly  12/12   (the record's own contribution)

Cutting the step to one column nearly doubles it. The model never performs multi-digit
arithmetic at all — only single-digit sums it has memorised — and the record carries
the carry. The oracle arm separates the two possible failures: at 12/12 the
decomposition itself is sound, so the one digitwise miss is a single-digit sum the
model got wrong, not a flaw in the split.

An eighth harness bug of the same family produced 0/12 on the first run. `-n 12` cut
the model off mid-restatement — "The total of the three digits 9, 7, and" — and the
extractor took the FIRST number, returning 9. Every digitwise answer came back as the
first operand, which looked exactly like a model that cannot add and was a token budget
of twelve. Fixed to n=48 and the last number.

================================================================================
PHASE 18 — two-way, held together by a residue
================================================================================

Generalising past arithmetic needs the atom and the state named for an arbitrary
procedure, and the examples that prompt it are not arithmetic: how do you build a
table, how do you open a bottle in the fridge and pour a glass. Those have a digit
column's shape — an action consumes conditions that must hold and produces conditions
that then hold — so the substitution is operands -> preconditions, values -> facts, and
nothing else in the record changes.

Forward-only made it worse, not better:

    whole plan in one go   2/3
    one action at a time   0/3     28 steps refused

Going forward greedily it picks "cut four legs" or "put teabag in cup" — locally legal,
globally stuck — because nothing forward-facing knows what the goal still needs. One
direction is not enough.

So run both, and let the record hold the difference. This is the codec contract:
base + residual == d, where the base is the approximation and the residual is the exact
correction that makes it lossless. The forward chain is the base — what is true so far;
the backward chain states what the goal requires; and

    residue = required - established

is the residual. Two-way stops being a heuristic under this rule, because the loop may
not terminate while the residue is non-zero and every accepted step must shrink it. The
model writes the reverse direction itself: it is never shown the dependency table, only
what is missing, and is asked what produces it.

NUMERIC. The model runs its own sum backwards by subtraction; the record subtracts
column by column. A known error is injected into one column first, because the
digitwise arm is already 11/12 and natural errors are too rare to measure the mechanism.

    error detected by the residue      12/12
    residue named the right column     11/12
    repaired by patching that column   11/12
    no error injected -> residue zero  11/12   (false-alarm control)

The residue does not merely say something is broken. It says where, which is what makes
a patch possible instead of a rewrite — the property the work record was built for and
had never been demonstrated on real model output.

PROCEDURAL. Backward regression from the goal, residue = preconditions still unmet:

    task                    free   forward-only   two-way + residue
    pour a glass of soda      .          .              ok
    build a table            ok          .               .
    make tea                 ok          .              ok

2/3, with both plans valid on forward replay, and it solves the one free planning
misses. They fail on different tasks, so the directions are not redundant.

The first procedural run reported 0/3 and it was mine, not the model's: on refusal the
loop reissued an identical prompt at temperature zero, so it received an identical
answer ten times and counted ten failures. Withdrawing a refused action from the menu is
what makes the retry a different question. Ninth bug of the family, and a pointed one —
the record's memory of what was tried is the thing that fixes it, and it had not been
wired into this arm.

What is established: the split that took addition from 6/12 to 11/12 is not arithmetic
machinery. It transfers to ordinary procedures under one substitution, and the residue
that makes a codec lossless is the same object that makes a two-way plan checkable.
What is not: build a table still fails, backward and forward, and three tasks is three
tasks.

================================================================================
PHASE 19 — step arity is not the lever, and the table was a matcher bug
================================================================================

Two questions, one run: fix the task the two-way arm could not plan, and test the claim
that a step should always be split two-into-one rather than many-into-one.

THE TABLE. Not a planning failure. Asked what makes the table stand, the model replied
"Attach legs to the top." — the menu says "attach legs to top", one article apart — and
the exact-substring matcher returned None, so it was counted as a refusal without the
record ever checking whether the action pointed the right way. Every run of that task
failed on an article. Matching on content words, with articles and prepositions
discarded, fixes it and the task now plans in every arm. Tenth harness bug of the same
family, and the same lesson each time: check the raw output before believing a zero.

ARITY, NUMERIC. A column of long addition is x + y + carry, three into one. Split into
two pairwise sums — (x + y), then (+ carry) — over 24 carry-heavy problems:

    three digits per step   23/24    72 calls
    two digits per step     22/24   108 calls

Splitting does not help. It costs 50% more calls and introduces two new errors
(355 + 764 -> 1219, 269 + 249 -> 528), because the extra intermediate is one more place
to misread. The phase 17 gain came from cutting multi-digit to single-digit, not from
cutting three inputs to two.

ARITY, PROCEDURAL. Sixteen everyday procedures, four of them built with three- and
four-way joins because the first twelve turned out to join at most two — the first run
of this comparison returned identical refusal counts on every task, which was not a
result but proof that both arms had run the same experiment. With real wide joins and a
step budget proportional to the graph:

    all open subgoals shown   11/16
    at most two shown         11/16
    identical outcome on      16/16 tasks

`lay a fire` and `pack for a trip` both join four preconditions and both plan correctly
under either arm. The arity claim is not supported in either domain.

WHAT DOES PREDICT FAILURE is size, not join width:

    tasks with >= 9 actions    1/5 solved
    tasks with <= 8 actions   10/11 solved
    widest join >= 3           2/5 solved
    widest join <= 2           9/11 solved

The join-width split is the weaker of the two and is confounded — the wide-join tasks
are also the big ones. `change a bicycle tyre` joins at most one precondition anywhere,
is ten actions deep, and fails; `pack for a trip` joins four and succeeds.

So the two levers this project has verified are the size of the atom and the residue
that checks it. How many things a single step combines is not one of them.

================================================================================
PHASE 20 — compress into a node, keep the residue, accept it only if it reverses
================================================================================

The rule under test: a substitution is a residue exactly as long as our formula can put
it back, because then the model may work on the compressed form and the reduction is
still safe. `X = (3726 + 52 * 4) / 526` becomes `X = Y / 526`, and neither half is an
approximation because the original is recoverable.

The record's check has two halves, and getting it wrong cost a whole run:

    text   reduced.replace(sym, part) == original      exact recovery
    value  eval(reduced with (part) put back) == eval(original)   precedence safety

The second half is not decoration. `37 + 29` is a perfectly good substring of
`37 + 29 * 2` and a ruinous residue, because `(37 + 29) * 2` is 132 and not 95. My first
version checked only the bracketed form against the original characters — and
`(37 + (29 * 2)) / 5` is not character-identical to `(37 + 29 * 2) / 5` — so it refused
every correct answer the model gave and the arm silently degraded into evaluating the
whole expression. Both arms returned identical answers on all twenty problems, which is
what gave it away. Two harness bugs before that: the extractor took the model's whole
sentence instead of the quoted subexpression, and a wait condition matched the previous
run's output file so I read stale results.

Twenty parenthesised four-term expressions, OLMoE 1B-7B:

    whole expression in one go        3/20
    compressed to nodes, residue back 2/20
    substitutions accepted            9/20 problems
    widest form the model ever held   8.3 tokens, down from 9.0

The mechanism works and does not pay. The reason is visible in the accepted nodes:
alongside `11 * 8` and `32 * 12` the record accepted `Y = 3` and `Y = 48`. Substituting
a bare number is perfectly reversible and completely useless, and the record has no
grounds to refuse it, because the rule it is enforcing is safety and not progress.

    Reversibility is necessary and not sufficient. It makes a substitution SAFE.
    It does not make it USEFUL.

That is the sharpened form of the rule, and 9.0 -> 8.3 tokens is its measurement: the
widest thing the model had to hold barely moved. What phase 17 had that this does not is
a compression that shrinks the dimension the model actually fails in — there, the atom
became a single-digit sum the model reliably knows. Here, deferring `38 + 39 * 8` out of
`(38 + 39 * 8) / 2` leaves a node that is still a precedence problem, which is the exact
thing the model was already failing. Making the compression recursive, so every node is
reduced to one operation, changed nothing, because the nodes the model proposed were
already small — its choices, not the recursion, were the limit.

The 3/20 baseline is also the honest caveat: this model cannot evaluate a four-term
expression at all, so there was very little for any decomposition to recover.

PLACEHOLDER. The same rule stated over preconditions instead of characters. "Provide
something to hang it on" is a legal step because it expands back; whether it turns out
to be a nail, a plug and hook, or an adhesive strip is a subproblem that need not be
settled to get the rest of the plan right. An expansion reverses when it consumes no
more than the abstract step consumed and delivers everything it promised:

    3/3 expansions restore the abstract step's contract

So the plan built around the placeholder stays valid whichever expansion is chosen
later. That is the procedural form of the residue rule, and unlike the numeric arm it is
a property of the graph rather than of the model, which is why it holds exactly.

================================================================================
PHASE 21 — the model writes only the links, as JSON, and the record owns the rules
================================================================================

Phase 20 failed on its interface, not on its idea. Naming a substring to defer is not
something these models are good at, and the substitutions it chose proved it: `Y = 3`,
perfectly reversible and compressing nothing. Emitting JSON is something they are good
at. So the model writes a graph and nothing else — `38 + 39 * 8` is A, and A can be
written in the next step — while every rule that makes it safe lives in the record:

    single assignment   a key is written once, never rewritten
    defined before use  a key must exist before it is referenced, no cycles
    at most three in    a step combines at most three numbers or earlier keys
    reversible          inline every key back and the value must equal the original

Same twenty expressions, same seed as phase 20, so all three arms compare directly:

    whole expression in one go             3/20
    substring substitution (phase 20)      2/20
    model writes the links as JSON        11/20

    parsed as a single-assignment object  20/20
    passed every rule including reversal  15/20
    mean 3.0 steps, widest step 3 inputs, 87% of inner keys used once onward

Nearly a fourfold gain from changing the interface alone. The model's arithmetic did not
improve and neither did the decomposition principle; what changed is that it was asked
for structure in a notation it can actually produce. 20/20 parsed — not one malformed
object, not one key written twice.

The two halves of the result are worth separating, because they are different claims.

WHAT THE MODEL CONTRIBUTES is the graph, and the shape it produces unprompted is the
shape that was wanted: three inputs at the widest, one result onward, 87% of inner keys
consumed exactly once. It was told "at most three" and never asked for fan-out of one,
and it wrote fan-out of one anyway.

WHAT THE RECORD CONTRIBUTES is five refusals out of twenty, and they are not
conservatism. Four inline to a different value — `{"A": "46", "B": "34 + 11",
"C": "B / 13"}` for `(46 + 34 + 11) / 13` silently drops the 46 — and one violates the
arity rule. A graph that inlines to the wrong value cannot produce the right answer, so
none of those refusals costs a correct result. The check is free in that direction.

Of the fifteen the record accepted, eleven are right. So the arm that only answers when
its decomposition verifies has 73% precision at a 75% claim rate, against a whole-
expression arm that always claims and is right 15% of the time. That is the comparison
phase 15 wanted and could not run: iterating does not destroy the answer here, because
what survives to be answered has been checked structurally first.

The remaining four failures are model arithmetic inside a structurally valid graph —
`(39 - 31 * 7) / 2` decomposes correctly and then the model evaluates a step wrong. That
is the floor the record cannot lift, and it is the same floor phase 17 hit: the fix for
it is a smaller atom, not a better graph.

================================================================================
PHASE 22 — the diff as the step, and the diff as an objective
================================================================================

Dropping the keys: the model rewrites the expression with one part replaced by its
value, and the record diffs consecutive states. No symbol table, no naming, and the new
number is itself the pointer back to the part it replaced. Single assignment comes free
because a state is never edited, only succeeded.

The legality rule became the residue rule stated over a transition — the new state must
evaluate to the same value, and be strictly shorter — after requiring one contiguous
replacement turned out to reject correct work: the model writes `(38 + 312) / 2` as
`(350 / 2)`, moving a bracket while it substitutes, which is a fine step.

    whole expression in one go   3/20
    first rewrite accepted       1/20
    record verifies and re-asks  5/20
    and breaks a stuck step down 5/20

Worse than the JSON graph's 11/20, so the interface still matters more than the loop.
But the failures are now attributable, which the JSON arm could not do:

     operator   wrong   right   error rate
            *       1      13         0.07
            -       0       2         0.00
            +       4       4         0.50
            /      15      11         0.58

Multiplication is nearly free and division is where it breaks, with both operands
two-digit at 0.58. Caveat: 32 rejected steps got no operator attributed because the diff
could not isolate one, so that part measures the extractor as much as the model.

DIFF SIZE AS A SIGNAL — RETRACTED. On four hand-picked cases it looked decisive: 9 and
19 characters when right against 32 and 30 when wrong. Over all 82 steps it is 10.4 when
right and 11.2 when wrong, and only 31% of wrong steps exceed the average right one. It
was a four-point impression, not a signal, and it was reported as one before the full run
existed.

================================================================================
PHASE 23 — the model cannot go backwards, unless it writes the question itself
================================================================================

The round trip needs the model to consume the residual it produced: hand it the answer,
not the problem, and have it invert each step. Agreement would then be a correctness
check needing no grader. It rests on an assumption, so the assumption was measured on
its own — the same 24 arithmetic facts asked in both directions.

    forward, plain                22/24
    forward, JSON                 17/24
    backward, blank-filling        5/24
    backward, blank-filling as JSON 5/24
    backward, posed as arithmetic  13/24

Backward is not the same task for this model. Blank-filling is a quarter of forward, and
JSON does not help it at all — while it actively hurts forward, which is worth noting
against the phase 21 result: JSON is not a magic notation, it helped there because
structure was what was being asked for.

Rephrasing the inversion as ordinary arithmetic recovers half the gap. That rewrite was
mine, though, and a table of inverse operators does not generalise. Handing the residual
to the model and asking it for a form it prefers does:

    answer the blank directly         5/24
    our rewrite into arithmetic      13/24
    THE MODEL WRITES THE QUESTION    19/24   (24/24 produced a valid question)
    as a word problem                10/24

Nearly four times the floor, and nothing in the code knows what an inverse is. It wrote
`What is 228 - 110?` and `What is 10206 / 27?` unaided. The word-problem framing is worse,
so the win is not "natural language" — it is that the model states the inverse better
than a lookup table does.

REPETITION DOES NOT HELP. At temperature zero a repeat is not a repeat, so five samples
were drawn at 0.8:

    one sample          13/24
    majority of five    13/24
    room to think       9/24

All five agreed on 22 of 24, and 59% of those were right against a 54% base rate. The
errors are systematic, not stochastic — the model is consistently wrong, not
occasionally wrong — so neither voting nor unanimity gives anything to act on, and
thinking longer makes it worse.

================================================================================
PHASE 24 — the floor, and why deepening cannot reach it
================================================================================

`228 - 110` is still three digits wide, and phase 17 measured that atom as too large. So
the rephrased question was broken down again, three ways:

    the model's own question, answered whole   16/24
    split into digit columns by the record     10/24   (8 of 24 had columns)
    the model splits it again as JSON           3/24

Deepening does not help, and the reason is the floor itself: asked to subtract two single
digits, the model answered `2 - 1` with 2. `8 - 0`, `5 - 0` and `9 - 3` are all correct,
so it is not the prompt. There is no smaller atom to break a single-digit subtraction
into, and no record can supply one.

A measurement caveat that applies to every number in this file: the same prompt at
temperature zero gave 19/24 in phase 23 and 16/24 here. Metal reductions are not
deterministic, so single runs carry roughly +/-3 of run-to-run variation and only the
larger gaps mean anything.

================================================================================
PHASE 25 — free-text editing is not a reversible operation
================================================================================

Arithmetic keeps hitting a floor nothing can lift, but the residue rule never needed
arithmetic — it needs an operation that undoes. Cutting a span of text out and naming it
looked ideal: "The fox is big" becomes "The A is big" with A = "fox", and pasting back
must give the original characters.

    produced a substitution        16/16
    and it pastes back exactly      5/16
    restored the original itself    8/16

It always answers and it usually corrupts. "The old bridge crosses the river" came back
as "The Aa crosses the Riva"; "She left her umbrella on the train" as "She Aave her
Ambrella on the Aain". Copying is not free for a 1B model, so an operation defined as
"emit the edited text" cannot be made reversible by checking it — it just fails the check.
